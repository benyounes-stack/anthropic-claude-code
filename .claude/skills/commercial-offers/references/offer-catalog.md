# Service catalog and scope writing

Pick services **backwards from the findings**, never forwards from this list. The list exists so you phrase a service well once you've decided it's justified — not so you can assemble a plausible-looking package.

Each entry below names the finding that justifies it. If you can't point at that finding in this client's data or in the interview, the service doesn't belong in this offer.

---

## Acquisition / media

**Pilotage média Meta** — daily buying, budget allocation, structure, testing.
*Justified by:* budget concentrated on underperformers, no test structure, plateaued spend, or simply an unmanaged account.
*Deliverables:* restructured account, defined testing cadence, weekly budget arbitration, monthly reporting.

**Extension multi-canal** (Google/PMax, TikTok, Pinterest, Amazon)
*Justified by:* Meta saturation — rising frequency, CPM up with flat volume — or an attribution breakdown showing a channel doing real work with no investment behind it.
*Careful:* a channel extension is a new cost centre with its own ramp-up. Only propose it with a budget and a learning window stated, or it becomes the thing that dilutes results.

**Restructuration de compte** (project)
*Justified by:* fragmented campaigns, overlapping audiences, budget scattered across ad sets that never exit learning.
*Deliverables:* target architecture, migration, before/after over a defined window.

**Scaling piloté**
*Justified by:* the profitable-and-scalable verdict — proven ROAS at a budget level that has never been raised.
*Deliverables:* ramp plan with steps and stop-loss rules, incrementality checks. Always with guardrails: uncontrolled scaling is the fastest way to destroy a good account and a good relationship.

## Creative

**Production créative** (static / UGC / video)
*Justified by:* spend concentrated on 1–2 creatives, creatives running unchanged for months (visible in the Ads Library even without account access), or CTR decaying at flat CPM.
*Deliverables:* a stated monthly volume by format, an angle/iteration framework, and who supplies the raw material — that last point causes more disputes than price.

**Stratégie d'angles / messaging**
*Justified by:* one angle carrying everything, or a mismatch between what the ads promise and what the site delivers.

**Refonte landing / PDP** (project)
*Justified by:* strong ad metrics with weak site conversion — the bottleneck is after the click, and buying more traffic makes it worse.

## Rétention / CRM

**Lifecycle email & SMS**
*Justified by:* low repeat rate, email/SMS a marginal share of revenue, missing or unmaintained flows.
*Deliverables:* named flows, campaign calendar, segmentation.
*Why it's usually the best line in a DTC offer:* the revenue it adds requires no additional media budget, which makes the payback arithmetic unusually easy to defend.

**Rétention / seconde commande**
*Justified by:* high new-customer share, long time-to-second-order.
*Deliverables:* post-purchase sequence, bundles/subscription, win-back campaigns.

## Mesure

**Tracking / CAPI / server-side**
*Justified by:* pixel errors, conversion gaps between platform and back-office, or decisions being made on platform-reported numbers alone.
*Sell it early:* it's cheap, obviously missing, and it makes every subsequent phase provable — which protects the agency as much as the client.

**Mise en place Triple Whale / attribution**
*Justified by:* no blended view, arguments about which channel deserves credit.

**Reporting & pilotage**
*Justified by:* every mandate. Keep it a supporting line, not a headline — nobody signs for a dashboard. Reference the `custom-reports` skill for the deliverable itself.

## Conseil

**Audit** (project, standalone)
*Justified by:* a cold prospect, or a situation too unclear to price a full mandate honestly. The best low-commitment entry point in the catalog.

**Consulting / accompagnement stratégique**
*Justified by:* an internal team that needs direction rather than execution. Priced by day or by month, never by "availability".

## Writing scope so it doesn't cause a dispute

**Be specific about volume and cadence.** "Gestion des campagnes" means nothing shared. "Pilotage quotidien, arbitrage budgétaire hebdomadaire, 8 nouveaux créatifs/mois, point mensuel de 60 min" means something both parties can check.

**Name what the client owes you.** Access, budget, product assets, validation deadlines, a single point of contact. A mandate that stalls because approvals take three weeks is a failure the client will still attribute to the agency.

**Always include an explicit exclusions list.** Media budget, third-party tools/subscriptions, photo/video shoots, site development, translation, additional channels — whatever applies. It reads as clarity, and it's the clause that saves the relationship in month four.

**Say what "done" looks like per phase.** Each phase needs an observable end state ("compte restructuré, tracking validé, 3 angles testés"), not a duration alone.

**Don't sell what the client can't absorb.** If capacity is the bottleneck (interview question 8), a scaling scope is a promise to break. Sell the constraint's fix, or sell less — an oversized offer that churns at month three costs more than a right-sized one that renews twice.

## Ordering the scope in the document

Order by the client's pain, not by your workflow. The first line should be the one that answers the sharpest finding in the diagnostic — that's the line that makes the reader think *ils ont compris mon problème*. Supporting lines (reporting, meetings, tooling) go last, grouped, without inflated importance.
