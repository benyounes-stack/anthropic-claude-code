# Seasonality — selling the right offer at the right moment

"An offer all year round" doesn't mean the same offer twelve times. The same scope, at the same price, is an easy yes in September and a hard no in December — because what the client is worried about changes every quarter.

Two rules drive everything here:

**Sell against the next peak, not the current month.** In August, a Q4 client isn't thinking about August. Anchoring the offer to what's coming makes the urgency real instead of manufactured.

**Respect lead times, and be honest when the window has closed.** Creative production, learning phases and tracking work take weeks. Selling a "Q4 play" starting November 15 sets up a failure you'll be blamed for. Say it: *"pour du Q4, il faut démarrer avant fin septembre — sinon on prépare janvier, qui est un meilleur point d'entrée."* Turning down the wrong timing wins more trust than accepting it.

Always verify against the client's **own** 12-month curve. The calendar below is a prior; their data is the fact. A B2B client peaks in September and dies in August; a pool-supply store inverts the entire retail calendar.

---

## The year

**January — "Q5"**
CPMs collapse after the Black Friday auction clears while post-holiday purchase intent stays high: often the year's best acquisition efficiency, and almost nobody plans for it.
*Offers that work:* immediate ramp-up ("le trimestre le moins cher de l'année, il reste 3 semaines"), annual planning, audits paid out of unused budget. New-year budgets unlock — the best month of the year to sign a 12-month mandate.

**February — reset**
Post-holiday slump, budgets set, decisions actually get made. Valentine's for gifting/jewelry/beauty.
*Offers:* the year's structural work — restructuring, tracking, CRM foundations. Nothing is on fire, so clients can think.

**March–April — spring ramp**
Genuine recovery. Easter, spring launches, garden/outdoor/sport categories wake up.
*Offers:* scaling and channel extension. Q1 results are in, so renewals and repricing land well.

**May–June — peak before the lull**
Mother's/Father's Day, end-of-season. Strong for many DTC categories.
*Offers:* pre-summer scaling, and **Q4 preparation sold early** — the highest-margin conversation of the year, because there's still time to do it properly and almost no competitor is having it yet.

**July–August — the lull (and the opportunity)**
Volume drops in many categories, CPMs soften, clients slow down. The classic mistake is cutting budget entirely.
*Offers:* everything that needs calm — audits, restructuring, tracking, creative production stockpiled for autumn, Q4 preparation as a named project with a September deadline. Also the best window for a **paid audit as an entry point**: the prospect has time to read it.
*Watch:* French/European clients disappear for most of August. Send offers before July 20 or after August 20.

**September — the real new year**
Everyone returns, budgets reopen, back-to-school. The last honest moment to start a Q4 mandate.
*Offers:* full-mandate acquisition with an explicit Q4 ramp plan. Urgency here is factual, which makes it persuasive: creative for November has to exist in October.

**October — pre-BFCM**
CPMs are already climbing. Preparation is the entire game: creative, offers, stock, audiences, tracking.
*Offers:* short, operational, deadline-driven — a "BFCM sprint" as a fixed-fee project converts well and doubles as an entry point into a January retainer.
*Be honest:* a mandate starting mid-October can prepare BFCM; it cannot fix a structurally broken account first.

**November — BFCM, the peak**
The auction is at its most expensive; the year's margin is made or lost in ten days. Nobody signs a new long-term mandate mid-month.
*Offers:* emergency/rescue only, or a "sortie de BFCM + janvier" offer sold in the last week, while the results are fresh and the client's memory of what went wrong is vivid.

**December — the run-out**
Pre-Christmas cutoff, then the post-Christmas/Boxing Day window that most brands under-exploit. Clients are exhausted and planning next year.
*Offers:* annual-review-plus-next-year-plan (a renewal in disguise, and a good one), sold on the year's data. Perfect timing for a January start.

**Category exceptions to hold in mind**
- **B2B / lead gen** — inverse curve: dead in July–August and late December, peaks in September–October and January–March. Never sell a B2B client on a Q4 retail narrative.
- **Muslim-market clients** (relevant to several accounts here): Ramadan and the two Eids move ~11 days earlier each year and completely reshape purchase behaviour — check the actual dates for the year in question rather than assuming.
- **France** — the regulated *soldes* windows (January and June/July) dominate fashion and retail.
- **Canada** — Boxing Day is a genuine second peak, unlike in France.
- **Subscription/SaaS** — mostly calendar-insensitive; driven by fiscal year and budget cycles instead. Don't force a seasonal narrative onto them.

## Using this in the offer

Two concrete places it shows up:

**Phasing.** Work backwards from the client's peak. If BFCM is the peak and it's July, phase 1 (audit + tracking) must complete in August, phase 2 (creative + structure) in September, phase 3 (ramp) from mid-October. Dates in an offer make it a plan; durations alone make it a wish.

**Urgency, when it's real.** "Il reste 9 semaines avant le lancement de la production créative pour Q4" is a fact and it moves decisions. Invented scarcity ("offre valable 48h") on a proposal of this size reads as a discount-store tactic and damages the positioning that the rest of the document worked to build. The offer validity date from `rate_card.json` is enough.
