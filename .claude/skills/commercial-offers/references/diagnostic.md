# Diagnostic — from data to a situation verdict

Two clients in the same industry, with the same budget, can need opposite offers. What decides the offer isn't the sector — it's the **economic situation**: whether the business is making money on paid, whether growth is coming from new or returning customers, whether the constraint is demand, margin, creative or capacity.

So before writing scope, name the situation. One verdict, in one sentence, that you could defend to the client's CFO.

## The four questions that produce the verdict

**1. Are they making money on paid, right now?**
Compare MER to breakeven MER (`1 / gross margin`). Above it with room to spare → the constraint is volume. Hovering at it → the constraint is efficiency. Below it → nothing else matters until that's fixed; selling scaling here is selling a faster way to lose money.
Without the client's margin you cannot answer this — which is why round 2 of the interview fights for that number.

**2. Where does the growth come from?**
New-customer share of revenue, repeat rate, email/SMS share, returning AOV. A business at 85% new-customer revenue is renting its growth from Meta and will stall the moment CPMs rise. One at 55% returning has a machine — and usually an under-exploited one.

**3. What's the direction, and is it structural or an event?**
Twelve months of monthly data separates trend from noise. A CPA that doubled in one month and returned is an event (stock-out, tracking break, seasonality). A CPA drifting up 8%/month for six months while CPM rises with it is auction pressure. A CPA drifting up while CPM is flat is creative fatigue or targeting decay — different problem, different offer.

**4. What is the actual bottleneck?**
Concentration tells you: spend on 2 creatives → creative production. 70% of budget on retargeting → prospecting is the bottleneck. High ROAS at low spend, never scaled → the bottleneck is nerve or budget, not skill. Strong ads with a bad AOV/refund profile → the bottleneck is offer/merchandising, and no amount of media buying fixes it.

## The recognizable situations

Each carries its own offer logic. Most clients are one of these plus a wrinkle.

**Profitable and scalable** — MER comfortably above breakeven, stable or improving trend, headroom in the account, capacity to ship.
→ Sell **scaling**: budget ramp with guardrails, creative volume to feed it, incrementality checks. This is the one situation where a performance-linked fee is genuinely attractive to both sides.

**Plateaued — spend flat, revenue flat** — the account has been at the same level for 6+ months.
→ Sell **unlocking**: new angles, new audiences, new placements/channel, offer restructuring. The diagnostic must name *why* it plateaued, or the offer reads as "the same thing but by us".

**Margin erosion — revenue holds, profit doesn't** — rising CPM/CPA, ROAS drifting down, discounting up, refunds up.
→ Sell **efficiency**: kill the loss-making segments, restructure the account, rebuild the creative pipeline, fix measurement. Lead with money recovered, not growth added — that's the number this client feels.

**Acquisition-dependent, no retention** — high new-customer share, weak repeat rate, email/SMS a rounding error.
→ Sell **the second purchase**: CRM/lifecycle, post-purchase flows, retargeting on owned data, bundles/subscription. Usually the single highest-ROI offer available, and the easiest to prove: the uplift needs no extra media budget.

**Structurally unprofitable on paid** — below breakeven MER even with generous assumptions.
→ Do **not** sell scaling. Sell a diagnostic/restructuring phase with a decision point: fix unit economics (price, AOV, COGS, offer) before spending more. Have the courage to write that the problem may not be the media buying — an offer that says so, backed by their own numbers, wins trust and often the deal. If the economics can't work at all, say the work isn't worth taking (see the walk-away price in the internal brief).

**Seasonal peak approaching** — the year's revenue concentrates in a window that's close (see `seasonality.md`).
→ Sell **preparation with a deadline**: the offer's urgency is real and datable, and phasing is everything. Be honest if the ramp-up window has already closed — promising a Q4 turnaround starting in November is how you inherit the blame.

**Account in trouble** — disabled/unsettled account, delivery errors, broken pixel/CAPI, policy rejections.
→ Sell **stabilization first**, as a short, cheap, fast-win phase, then the real mandate. Nothing else in the offer is credible while the account can't deliver.

**Data-blind** — no attribution tool, broken tracking, decisions made on platform-reported numbers only.
→ Sell **measurement** as phase 1. It's an easy yes (it's cheap and obviously missing) and it makes every later phase provable, which protects the user as much as the client.

## Turning the verdict into the offer's opening

The client reads the first page and decides whether the rest is worth reading. That page is the verdict, stated as **an observation with a number attached and a consequence**, never as a judgment:

> **Observed:** 38% du budget des 90 derniers jours est allé sur des campagnes au-dessus du CPA cible.
> **Consequence:** environ 14 200 $ de budget à récupérer par trimestre, à volume constant.
> **Therefore:** phase 1 = restructuration du compte, avant toute augmentation de budget.

Three lines, three roles: what you saw, what it costs them, what you propose. If a scope item in your offer doesn't have a chain like that behind it, it isn't justified — cut it.

## Sanity checks before you commit to a verdict

- **One month is not a trend.** Check whether the pattern holds across at least three periods.
- **Check the denominator.** A ROAS drop with spend up 4× is a scaling story, not a decline.
- **Check the calendar.** Compare year-over-year, not just month-over-month, on any seasonal business — otherwise you'll diagnose January as a collapse every single year.
- **Check attribution before diagnosing a "drop".** An iOS/tracking/attribution-window change moves reported numbers without anything real changing.
- **Ask before accusing.** Anything that looks like an obvious mistake gets question 7 of the interview first. The client — or the user — may have had a reason, and a confident wrong diagnostic in a proposal is unrecoverable.
